## What this notebook covers

TF-IDF (Term Frequency — Inverse Document Frequency) fixes the biggest flaw of Bag-of-Words: it down-weights common words and up-weights rare, informative ones. You will implement TF-IDF from scratch, then verify it matches scikit-learn's `TfidfVectorizer`, and visualise how rankings shift compared to raw counts.

## What the previous approach got wrong

BoW gives equal weight to every word. A word like "the" that appears in every document contributes as much as a rare, meaningful word. This makes search results noisy and poorly ranked.

## Key concept

**TF-IDF** = Term Frequency × Inverse Document Frequency

- **TF**: how often does this word appear in *this* document?
- **IDF**: how rare is this word across *all* documents? (log scale)
- Words that are frequent in one document but rare across the corpus get high scores — exactly what good retrieval needs.

## Limitation this notebook reveals

TF-IDF still knows nothing about meaning. "forgiveness" and "pardon" are completely unrelated in TF-IDF space — they share no characters, so they share no score. A query for "mercy" will never retrieve a document that only uses the word "compassion", even if they are semantically identical.

That is the problem word embeddings solve in NB04.
