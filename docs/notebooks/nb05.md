## What this notebook covers

FastText extends Word2Vec by representing each word as a sum of its character n-gram vectors. This means the model can construct vectors for words it has never seen during training — solving the out-of-vocabulary (OOV) problem that breaks Word2Vec in production.

## What the previous approach got wrong

Word2Vec fails completely on unknown words. If a user queries with a typo, a rare word, or a morphological variant the model was never trained on, the result is an error or a zero vector. This is a critical production failure mode.

## Key concept

**FastText subword model**: every word "mercy" is also represented by its character n-grams: `<me`, `mer`, `erc`, `rcy`, `cy>`, etc. The final word vector is the sum of all its character n-gram vectors. Unknown words can be approximated by combining their character n-grams.

```python
from gensim.models import FastText

ft = FastText(sentences, vector_size=100, window=5, min_n=3, max_n=6, **FT_KW)

# Works even for words never seen in training
ft.wv["zzzmercylikezzz"]  # OOV word — no error
```

## Limitation this notebook reveals

FastText is still a word-level model. Even with subword representations, you are averaging or combining word vectors to get a sentence representation. The model has no understanding of sentence structure, grammar, or how the meaning of words shifts in context.

Sentence embeddings in NB06 solve this with a model trained specifically to encode whole sentences.
