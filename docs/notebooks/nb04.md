## What this notebook covers

The leap from sparse to dense. Word2Vec trains a neural network to predict words from context (or context from words), producing 100-dimensional vectors where semantically similar words cluster together. You will train both CBOW and Skip-Gram models, compare them side by side, and visualise the learned space with PCA and t-SNE.

## What the previous approach got wrong

N-grams and TF-IDF use sparse, high-dimensional vectors where every word is orthogonal to every other. "mercy" and "compassion" share zero features. There is no notion of similarity between words — only exact overlap.

## Key concept

**Word2Vec**: train a shallow neural network on a prediction task (predict the centre word from context, or predict context from the centre word). The learned weights become the word vectors. Words used in similar contexts end up with similar vectors.

```python
from gensim.models import Word2Vec

# CBOW: predict centre word from context
cbow = Word2Vec(sentences, vector_size=100, window=5, sg=0, **W2V_KW)

# Skip-Gram: predict context from centre word
skipgram = Word2Vec(sentences, vector_size=100, window=5, sg=1, **W2V_KW)
```

## Limitation this notebook reveals

Word2Vec produces one vector per word. "bank" has one vector regardless of whether it means a river bank or a financial institution. And there is still no sentence-level representation — to represent a sentence you have to average word vectors, which loses a lot of information.

FastText in NB05 handles morphology better. Sentence embeddings in NB06 solve the sentence-level problem properly.
